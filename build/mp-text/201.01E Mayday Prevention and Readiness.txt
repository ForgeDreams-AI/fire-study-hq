PURPOSE

In firefighting, prevention is the cornerstone of safety, while Mayday readiness
ensures we are prepared if prevention measures fail. Readiness is critical for
response, but prevention keeps us out of high-risk scenarios. Focusing on
prevention reduces the likelihood of a Mayday and enhances safety for all
personnel.

Every firefighter must maintain Mayday readiness and adopt a proactive approach
to prevention. Prevention requires disciplined decision-making, strict adherence to
operational standards, and best practices. Key preventive actions include
operating within the Incident Command System (ICS), effective communication for
situational awareness, accountability, air management, and resource layering for
tactical reserves. These actions are more than protocols; they are critical steps that
keep us safe.

Embedding these practices into daily routines and fostering a mindset that
anticipates a Mayday builds a culture of safety prioritizing prevention as the first
line of defense. Readiness remains essential, but prevention is the key to
minimizing Mayday incidents and ensuring safe, effective operations.

operating within the Phoenix Regional Dispatch System, including:

DEFINITIONS

Mayday
A Mayday is defined as anytime a firefighter is in a life-threatening situation and
requires assistance because they cannot safely exit the hazard zone or resolve the
problem immediately.
Mayday Readiness
The state of being prepared to act promptly and effectively in the event of a
Mayday situation. This includes maintaining proficiency in essential survival skills,
equipment use, and communication under extreme conditions.

GRAB LIVES
A nationally recognized mnemonic for firefighter self-survival actions during a
Mayday:

   •   G = Check Air Gauge
   •   R = Radio for Assistance
   •   A = Activate PASS Device
   •   B = Control Breathing, Conserve Air
   •   L = Stay Low
   •   I = Illuminate, Turn Flashlight On
   •   V = Make Loud Noises (Volume)
   •   E = Find an Exit
   •   S = Shield Your Airway (last ditch effort)

Fireground Survival
A comprehensive training program sponsored and taught by the International
Association of Fire Fighters (IAFF). It focuses on firefighter self-rescue techniques,
air management, and survival strategies in zero-visibility or life-threatening
conditions.

Save Our Own
A set of procedures and skills, also sponsored and taught by the IAFF, focused on
rescuing firefighters in distress. This includes rapid intervention tactics and
specialized equipment use.

Incident Command System (ICS)
A standardized approach, as outlined in MP 201.01 Command Procedures, is
designed to enable effective and efficient on-scene incident management by
integrating organizational functions, tactical operations, incident planning,
incident logistics, and administrative tasks within a common organizational
structure.

Strategic Decision Making Model (SDM)

The process of an on-going size-up of fireground factors, assessment of risk,
determination of strategy, and development of an Incident Action Plan to complete
the Tactical Objectives. This process should be constantly reviewed and revised.
MP 201.01 Command Procedures

Risk Management Plan

A standardized approach for evaluating hazards, assessing risk, and applying
strategies to minimize exposure and prevent firefighter injury or death. This plan is
based on principles in what exactly firefighters will risk saving and is outlined in
MP 201.01C Risk Management and Safety.


     1. We Will risk our lives a lot, in a calculated manner, to save savable lives.

     2. We Will risk our lives a Little, in a calculated manner, to save savable
     property.

     3. We Will Not risk our lives at all for lives or property that are already Lost.

MAYDAY READINESS

To achieve Mayday readiness, all firefighters must consider every incident from
the standpoint of “What if I or another firefighter gets into trouble?” and “Am I
prepared to respond to a Mayday scenario?” All firefighters must be prepared to
act promptly in the event of a Mayday situation. The NFPA 1500 Standard defines
Mayday readiness as the ability to “don, doff, and manipulate the SCBA in zero
visibility while wearing firefighting gloves.” All firefighters must be proficient in
“Fireground Survival and Save our Own” procedures. These skills should be
practiced and trained on regularly to ensure Mayday Readiness.

The Rule of Mayday Readiness

Under the “Rule of Mayday Readiness,” all fire department personnel operating in
or around a hazard zone are responsible for maintaining a high level of readiness
in the event of a Mayday situation. This includes the ability to communicate and
respond effectively in a Mayday scenario, as well as everyday preparation and
prevention. Every member must carry their assigned functioning radio and be
proficient in its use—even in low-visibility or zero-visibility conditions—since
effective communication is critical to survival and rescue efforts. This diligent
approach ensures Mayday readiness, instilling a sense of preparedness and
confidence in our abilities.

“Mayday” procedures and actions are perishable for all levels of an organization.
Personnel should regularly train and simulate them. The perishable nature of
these procedures emphasizes the need for continuous training and improvement,
which should be developed into everyday activities and practiced at the Strategic,
Tactical, and Task levels.

MAYDAY PREVENTION

Effectively preventing Mayday situations involves multiple factors. Command
Officers, Company Officers, Engineers, and Firefighters must reinforce the
prevention of scenarios that lead to a Mayday. One of the most effective ways to
prevent a Mayday is to extinguish the fire quickly and aggressively, while
operating in a safe and calculated manner. Rapid fire control reduces hazards,
limits fire growth, and minimizes the exposure of crews operating inside the
hazard zone.

The most critical factors for Mayday prevention begin with the following
operational standards:

   •   Working within an Incident Command System
   •   Continual use of the Strategic Decision Making Model,
         o Using knowledge, skills, experience, and reconnaissance to
             continually size up the hazard zone

Readiness and Prevention - Company Officers

The Company Officer is responsible for Crew development and practice.
Consistent training and practice in Mayday prevention are crucial for a rapid and
appropriate response in an actual Mayday situation. This involves establishing a
consistent attitude and setting clear expectations for all crew members regarding
Mayday readiness. It also consists in developing routines that the company officer
encourages and participates in. The Company Officer must be prepared and
consider their strategic, tactical, and task actions.

Mayday Readiness and Prevention for Company Officers include:

   •   Ensure all members perform a daily SCBA operational check at the
       beginning of their shift.
   •   Practicing crew communication in the hazard zone
   •   Integrating rovers and constant staffing members into the crew
   •   Maintaining knowledge of portable radios and practicing their use
   •   Ensuring Company Officer responsibilities and expectations are clear to the
       entire crew
   •   Practicing and maintaining crew integrity/accountability
   •   Maintaining contact with a hoseline is a primary method of preventing a
       Mayday and is a required practice during interior fire operations. In certain
       residential incidents, operating off the hoseline may be appropriate;

Mayday Readiness and Prevention – Firefighter/Engineer

Every firefighter is responsible for actively and routinely doing their part to
prevent Mayday situations and be ready to respond to one. To increase their
chances of survival, members should focus on improving their knowledge, skills,
and abilities. The development of fireground discipline and survival skills begins at
the recruit level and should continue throughout a firefighter’s career.

Individual firefighter responsibilities for Mayday prevention include the following:

       •   Keeping current and invested in “Fireground Survival” techniques
       •   Perform daily SCBA operational check
       •   Maintaining SCBA proficiency
       •   Complete annual SCBA Mask fit test (MP 110.19)
       •   Practicing individual air management (task level of M.P. 202.05C)
       •   Use and care of personal protective equipment
       •   Maintaining portable radio proficiency and use
       •   Practicing crew communications
       •   Exercising fireground discipline
       •   Maintaining crew integrity
       •   Individual rovers and Constant Staffers are responsible for asking if
           expectations are not explicitly discussed at the beginning of a shift.
       •   Engineers shall Tag hose lines and provide tools at ingress points
