             PERMIT REQUIREMENTS

 M.P. 102.07A                                             Date Revised: 11/2026

POLICY HIERARCHY & PURPOSE
This procedure implements City Administrative Regulations (ARs) governing overnight vehicle permits
and operation, including:
    • AR 2.97 — Annual and Temporary Overnight Vehicle Permits, Monthly Reporting, and Motor
        Pools.
    • AR 2.95 — Motor Vehicle Authorization and Operation.
    • AR 2.29 — Communications and Transportation Expense Allowances.
    • AR 2.96 — Driver Qualification.
    • AR 6.11 — Fleet Standards and Utilization.

In the event of any conflict, the ARs control. Where this MP sets standards that are more restrictive than
the ARs, the stricter standard applies, provided it does not contradict the ARs. The purpose of this MP is
to translate AR requirements into Fire-specific roles, workflows, and controls to ensure compliant,
efficient, and safe use of City vehicles kept off-shift or at alternate locations.

SCOPE
Employees of the Fire Department who have a documented operational need for a City vehicle after
normal business hours may be authorized to keep a City vehicle overnight at an employee’s residence or
alternate location. Authorization must follow AR 2.97 and this MP. Overnight storage is intended to
facilitate emergency response and other official City business — not personal convenience.

DEFINITIONS
    •   Annual Permit (AR 2.97): Continuing, Rotating, or Report-to-Work-Site.
    •   Temporary Permit (AR 2.97): Valid for 1–30 days for specific business needs.
    •   Sworn Public Safety Staff: Sworn Fire personnel; subject to AR 2.97 exemptions on monthly
        reporting.
    •   Professional Staff: Non-sworn Fire personnel.
    •   Department Head/Designee: Fire Chief or authorized executive designee.
    •   Fleet Services: Public Works Department Fleet Services Division.
    •   Fire Fleet Unit: Fire’s internal fleet management unit.
    •   Resource Management: Resource Management is responsible for centralized oversight.

Permit Eligibility & Types

All employees keeping City vehicles overnight must hold an approved AR 2.97 permit:
    • Annual (Long-term): A. Continuing Permit. B. Rotating Permit. C. Report-to-Work-Site Permit.
    • Temporary (Short-term) — for 1–30 days.

Eligibility constraints:
    • Employees receiving a transportation allowance are not eligible to apply for any overnight permit
         (AR 2.97 referencing AR 2.29).
    • Employees who receive a transportation allowance will not have a City vehicle assigned to
         them, nor will they be allowed to use a City vehicle on a regular and ongoing basis. The vehicle
         allowance compensates employees for using their personal cars on official City business.
             o Battalion Chiefs and Shift Commanders (56-hour classifications) shall not have City
                 vehicles assigned to them. They will use personal vehicles when necessary for required
                 city business, when not on duty, and are therefore eligible for a transportation allowance.
                 While on duty, they will use City vehicles as required to fulfill their duties.
             o District Commanders (40-hour classifications) may choose to have a City vehicle
                 assigned to them, will be eligible for an overnight permit, and will not receive a
                 transportation allowance.
             o District Commanders (40-hour classifications) may choose not to have a City vehicle
                 assigned to them; in that case, they may only drive response vehicles to emergency
                 incidents, with no exceptions. Response vehicles are not permitted to be used for
                 transportation to meetings, training, or any other purpose other than responding from the
                 district office to emergencies; no exceptions.
    • Only employees qualified to drive per AR 2.96 may operate City vehicles.

Form Requirements & Versions

    •   Form 150-83D (Rev 5/23): Annual Permit to Keep Vehicle Off-Shift or in Alternate Location.
    •   Form 150-21D (Rev 5/23): Temporary Permit to Keep Vehicle Off-Shift or in Alternate Location.
    •   Form 150-84D (Rev 5/23): Employee’s Monthly Report of City Vehicle Under Annual or
        Temporary Permit (Non-exempt qualified personal use vehicles only).
    •   Employees must carry the approved paper or digital permit while operating the vehicle (AR 2.97).

Electronic routing & records:
    • Permit applications and approvals shall be routed and stored electronically using Fire’s designated
        platform, PPE Software. The official permit must be accessible with the vehicle (physical or
        electronic copy per City guidance).
    • The Fire Department will maintain a central master list of active permits and vehicle
        assignments (see Quarterly Review & Reporting).

Employee and Supervisor Responsibilities

    •   All employees in driving positions shall, once each calendar year, acknowledge their
        understanding of AR 2.95, AR 2.97, and MP 102.07A via PHXYou. Completion is tracked and
        retained in accordance with the City's record retention policy. Employees who have not
        completed the annual policy review and acknowledgment are prohibited from driving city
        vehicles.
    •   Supervisors are responsible for monitoring vehicle assignments and detecting unallowable usage.
        Section heads are responsible for the central tracking of all staff vehicle assignments, and
        periodic inspections shall be conducted.

   •   Prior to using a pool vehicle, the vehicle will be signed in and out, with a log of the business
       justification and supervisor pre-approval. These written reports shall be logged in PPE Software
       for the section head's monthly review and certification.
   •   Section heads must review and certify monthly compliance with permit use, locations, and city
       business purposes.
   •   Supervisors shall verify all mandatory permit fields (permit expiration, statement of need,
       overnight parking location, estimated uses/mileage, off-street parking, and required signatures)
       before approval. Any incomplete permit is invalid and must be resubmitted within five (5)
       business days.

Location & Security Requirements

   •   Vehicles must be parked in a secure, off-street location whenever available and feasible.
   •   Equipment and sensitive materials must be secured per Fire policy and AR 2.95.
   •   Overnight parking outside City limits is permitted only with prior written approval by the Fire
       Chief or designee and must be documented in the permit file. Extra justification is required when
       the overnight location is outside city limits. A memo is required with the permit so that leadership
       can evaluate whether it is in the City’s best interest (AR 2.97).

Approval Chain

   •   Assistant Chief approval is required on permit applications.
   •   Fire Chief or designee grants final approval.
   •   Approvals must be documented on the City forms (Rev 5/23) and in the electronic workflow PPE
       Software.

Quarterly Review & Reporting To Fire Fleet Unit/Fleet Services

Department-level responsibility:
   • Fire will maintain a list of authorized overnight permits and review usage quarterly for:
       continuing need, compliance with AR 2.95/2.97, accident information in the M5 system, and
       miles/trips (as applicable).
   • After each quarterly review, Fire will email the updated list to Public Works Fleet Services
       at pwd.fsdfleet.con@phoenix.gov (AR 2.97).
   • The Fire Fleet Unit coordinates the quarterly review and preparation of the list; Resource
       Management validates completeness; executive leadership approves transmission.
   • Fire will notify Fleet Services via PPE Software with changes to overnight parking locations to
       keep records current.

Monthly Reporting

   •   Sworn Fire personnel are exempt from the monthly mileage reporting requirement (AR 2.97)
   •   All other permit holders must submit Form 150-84D to the Department Payroll
       Representative by the first business day of the month for the prior month’s activity (AR 2.97).
   •   Note: If City Finance/Payroll updates Form 150-84D or directs a different submission date (e.g.,
       “second Monday”), Fire will follow that City directive and update this MP accordingly.
   •   Section Heads must certify monthly that vehicle use under their span of control complies with
       AR 2.95/2.97 and this MP; certifications are retained with payroll submissions.
   •   Failure to submit timely reports may result in permit revocation (AR 2.97).

Pool Vehicles

   •   Motor pool use is intended for short-term City business (generally no more than one shift).

Vehicle Operation Requirements

   •   When operating City vehicles (including under permits), employees must comply with AR 2.95,
       including—but not limited to—safe operation, distracted driving restrictions, seatbelt use,
       parking, inspections, collision reporting, and no expectation of privacy.
   •   Misuse may result in discipline up to and including dismissal (AR 2.95).

Roles & Accountabilities

   •   Fire Fleet Unit: Administers permit workflows; coordinates quarterly reviews; maintains master
       list. Vehicles shall not be issued to employees without confirmed compliance with City ARs and
       this procedure. Fire HR must be notified within two business days of any vehicle assignment
       addition/change/removal.
   •   Resource Management: Owns centralized oversight; ensures compliance tracking and reporting.
   •   Section/Division Management: Ensures accurate permits; verifies completeness before approval;
       reinforces compliance; escalates issues.
   •   Fire HR: Coordinates transportation allowance checks (AR 2.29) and driver qualification (AR
       2.96); ensures permit eligibility; manages payroll reporting intake.
   •   Fire HR and OSI: Upon receipt of driver record issues from the employee, supervisor, and/or City
       Safety through the PMG Driver’s License Report (e.g., criminal moving violations, DUI,
       cancellation/suspension/revocation), Fire HR and OSI will triage within (2) business days. All
       steps and timestamps shall be tracked in the Investigation/Discipline Tracker and routed per
       jurisdiction (professional staff vs. sworn). Professional staff MVD violations will be tracked in an
       investigation/discipline spreadsheet maintained by the Fire Human Resource Supervisor and
       Resource Officer. All sworn MVD violations will be tracked in the current Image Trend RMS
       Activity Log. The Deputy Chief, OSI, and the HR Supervisor will confirm that all reported and
       flagged MVD reports are properly routed, logged, and managed within AR 2.96 Driver
       Qualification.

Systems & Data

   •   PPE Software shall track permits, assignments, locations, quarterly reviews, and reporting
       schedules.
   •   PHXYou and PPE Software will track completions, generate reports, and retain records per City
       retention schedules.
   •   Integrate with M5 (via Public Works Fleet Services) for parking locations and vehicle data.
   •   Retain records per City record retention policies.

Central Vehicle-Assignment Monitoring

   •   The Fire Fleet Unit will maintain a centralized, historical master file of all Fire vehicle
       assignments and changes, separate from permits.
   •   The file is reviewed monthly; exception reports (e.g., vehicles without permits, anomalous use)
       are generated and escalated to division management.

Compliance, Enforcement & Exceptions

   •   Permits may be revoked at any time at the discretion of the Fire Chief, designee, or City Manager
       (AR 2.97).

   •   Exceptions/waivers must be documented in writing by the Fire Chief or designee and retained
       with the permit record.
