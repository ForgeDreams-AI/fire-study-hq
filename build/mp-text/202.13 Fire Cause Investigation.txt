PURPOSE

The goal of the Phoenix Fire Department Investigations Section is to provide quality
service in the investigation of fires and certain explosion incidents. The primary
responsibility of the Phoenix Fire Department Investigation Unit is to attempt to
determine the origin and cause of fires and to identify factors or persons responsible for
causing a specific fire.

The section serves effectively by analyzing fire statistics, recognizing fire trends to
efficiently protect lives and property, referring arson cases to the City or County
Attorney’s Office for prosecution, identifying Youth Firesetters and preventing fires
through education, code enforcement, standards and training.

Fire Cause Investigation

I. Fire cause determination should be attempted at all incidents; however, not all fires
   require a Fire Investigator. The initial responsibility lies with the incident commander
   on scene, who will determine—based on the available information and
   circumstances—whether to request a Fire Investigator. The on-duty Fire
   Investigators can be contacted via Dispatch and Deployment 24 hours a day or by
   phone to consult with Command prior to or during response to assist Command. A
   Fire Investigator should be contacted if the on-scene officer is unsure if an
   investigator is required.

    To allow for a comprehensive investigation any investigation should be performed
    after fire control activities, immediate salvage, but prior to removing contents and
    overhaul actions, which could hinder the investigation.

    An investigator should be dispatched by Alarm or called by the officer in charge and
    will respond to the scene in the following circumstances:

          o Fire deaths or serious fire injuries, including death and serious injury
            to firefighters

          o Attempted arsons

          o All working greater alarm structure fires

          o Fires for which the cause cannot be determined by Command on the
            scene

          o Explosions and bombings

The following are examples of when a Fire Investigator is not required to perform an
investigation. If an investigator is not requested, the company officer is responsible
for the completion of an accurate Field Incident Report (F.I.R.) to document the fire
cause:


      o Fires where cause is known to be accidental.

      o Scalding burns, electrical accidents, and minor accidental burn
        injuries.

      o Car fires originating in engine area, or abandoned autos, or vehicle
        fires with no identified suspect in custody

      o Grass, fence, or trash fires with no identified suspect in custody. (If
        multiple small fires are found but no one is around to provide
        information, the company officer will enter an F.I.R.) A follow-up call
        can be made to Fire Investigations at the company officer's
        discretion.

      o Traffic accident fatalities involving fire subsequent to collision are
        investigated by the Phoenix Police Department.

      o Known juvenile fire setters who have caused minor fires by playing
        with fire, matches, etc., can be referred to the Youth Firesetter
        Program. The Juvenile Assistance Referral Form can be accessed
        and completed on the Phoenix Fire Department intranet via FireWire
        or by calling the Juvenile Firesetter Specialist at 262-6774.

      o To hold the scene until another agency or service responds

      o When requested to make contact with homeowner for the purpose
        of insurance matters

II. If the cause can be determined easily by the Company Officer on the scene, and
    doesn’t meet the criteria to require a fire investigation response, through
    information received or by investigations of the fire scene (e.g. a stove fire with food
    remains on stove), units on the scene shall gather the information needed and
    accurately complete the required F.I.R. If a customer requests information regarding
    their fire loss, for insurance purposes, the company officer can give the fire
    department incident number, which will enable the victim to access fire department
    information regarding the fire.

III. When a Fire Investigator is on the scene or responding, companies shall delay non-
     essential overhaul and secure the fire scene until the investigator arrives. Salvage
     and all unnecessary interim activities which may alter or contaminate the fire scene,
     or which may interfere with a subsequent origin and cause investigation must be
     discontinued until authorized to continue by the responding Fire Investigator.

   Command shall assign personnel to protect the fire scene and maintain custody of
   the scene until the arrival of a Fire Investigator, especially when delay in response to
   the scene by an Investigator has been indicated.

   After achieving fire control, Command shall release companies not required to
   complete investigation and overhaul. In some cases, involving lengthy investigation
   periods, companies may return to quarters and return later to the scene to complete
   overhaul activities when requested by the on-scene Fire Investigators.

   Command shall turn over jurisdiction of the fire area to the Investigator as soon as
   possible after the fire is stabilized. The Fire Investigator retains jurisdiction until the
   Investigator releases it back to Command. The Investigator may inform Command
   that they intend to maintain custody of the scene for further investigation.

   The Fire Investigator shall request from Command any personnel or equipment
   necessary for the investigation. Command shall make every attempt to meet such
   requests, to the extent possible under the prevailing circumstances.

IV. All personnel shall cooperate with the Fire Investigator and with the Fire
    Investigations Sector. Protection of the fire scene and preservation of physical
    evidence must be a primary concern once life safety is secured, and fire control is
    achieved. An attempt to identify the responsible party and any witnesses who are at
    the fire scene must be made as soon as possible and shall not be delayed until the
    investigator is on the scene. Obtaining the identification of witnesses is critical to the

V. Destruction of Evidence: It is incorrect to believe that evidence is completely
   destroyed by fire. This misconception is the reason many incendiary fires are not
   prosecuted.

  Evidence is not destroyed in fires except in very rare cases. The form, shape, color,
  size, and weight may be altered, but evidence can still be identified upon trained
  examination. It is critically important to preserve evidence by not moving fire debris
  unnecessarily.

  The fire department must protect the fire scene from unnecessary damage during
  firefighting operations. Special care must be exercised during extinguishment to
  avoid washing out evidence through misuse of fire streams, or excessive foam
  application. Overhaul presents the greatest potential for damage to evidence, which
  may be used by the fire investigator and police department in subsequent court
  cases and prosecution.
  Salvage operations should be minimal until the investigation can be completed and
  should be confined to diminishing eventual loss. Companies assigned to Loss
  Control Sector should incorporate scene security and evidence preservation into
  their plan to stop the loss.

  The fire scene is the Investigator's laboratory. They must search it carefully and
  thoroughly, photograph the scene in place, diagram all evidence placement, and
  collect and preserve all evidence.

  The fire scene must be guarded. Evidence cannot be used in court unless the
  investigator can establish a chain of custody by proving who found the evidence,
  where it was found and prove that evidence was not tampered with while in official
  custody. To ensure that the chain of custody remains unbroken, the scene must
  remain demonstrably in the custody of the fire department. Thus, at a fire scene
  where it has been determined that custody must be maintained until the scene is
  released, only authorized persons may enter the fire scene.

  The fire department has the legal authority to close the scene entirely, even to the
  property owner or to other interested person/s. It is vital that the fire department
  prevent personnel from unnecessarily walking through a fire area, walking on,
  obscuring evidence, or picking up and moving evidence. This shall include both fire

Joint Fire/Police Investigations at Fire Scenes.

The Phoenix Fire Department Fire Investigations Task Force consists of Fire Captains,
both Arizona Peace Officer Standards and Training (AZPOST) certified and non-post
certified, and Phoenix Police Detectives.

Phoenix Fire Department Fire investigators are trained and certified as specialty peace
officers with limited authority to investigate arson crimes. This training and certification
meets the requirements of the Arizona Peace Officers Standards and Training Board.

Phoenix Police and fire department Investigators will conduct joint investigation of fire
deaths or life-threatening injuries at fire scenes:

    o    Fire Department Investigators are responsible for determining the fire
         origin and cause.

    o    Phoenix Police are responsible for the follow up investigation of all fires
         where arson is the cause.

    o    Phoenix Police are responsible for conducting the death investigation
         related to a fire fatality or unnatural death.

    o    Phoenix Police will coordinate with the Maricopa County Office of the
         Medical Examiner to determine the cause and manner of death.
